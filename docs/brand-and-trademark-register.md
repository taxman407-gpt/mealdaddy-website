# MealDaddy brand and trademark register

Status: owner-approved working register as of October 4, 2026, pending formal clearance and counsel review. This document records intended brand use; it is not a legal clearance opinion or proof of registration.

## Use rules

- Use `™` on the first prominent appearance of an unregistered claimed mark on a screen or page. Repeating it on every occurrence is unnecessary and visually distracting.
- Never use `®` unless the exact mark has a current federal registration covering the relevant goods or services.
- Use each mark consistently as a brand name, not as a generic noun or verb.
- Keep dated screenshots, releases, marketing pages, and repository history as evidence of actual use.
- Obtain a comprehensive federal, state, common-law, domain, app-store, and international clearance search before filing or expanding internationally.

## Retired or rejected working names

- `Nutrition Tracking Engine™` is retired as a claimed mark. Nutrition calculation remains a product capability described in ordinary language.
- `Adaptive Hydration™` is replaced by `MealDaddy Hydration Guardrails™`.
- `Goal-to-Table Loop™` is replaced by `MealDaddy GoalFlow™`.

### Approved GoalFlow presentation

**MealDaddy GoalFlow™**

Set goals → Plan → Log → Review → Learn → Plan again → Learn More
- `MenuMotive`, `MealLedger`, and `PlateFuse` were screening candidates only. MealDaddy did not adopt or use them as product marks.

## Approved presentation: One Meal, One Entry™

**One Meal, One Entry™**

Your photograph and clarification belong together.

A meal photograph creates one written entry. Measurements, ingredients, and preparation notes clarify the pictured food rather than becoming additional entries.

## Descriptive labels currently left unclaimed

Restaurant Mode, Reports, Weight & Progress, Today, Entries, Plan Your Next Meal, My Saved Favorites, and Feedback Loop remain ordinary feature labels unless counsel recommends a more distinctive composite mark. This avoids an app crowded with weak claims.

For each release, preserve the release date, commit identifier, representative screenshots, public URL, exact mark spelling, feature description, and the goods or services offered. Record the first verified public-use date only from evidence; do not infer or backdate it.
